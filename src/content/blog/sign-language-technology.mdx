Technology can help make communication more accessible when it is designed with Deaf and hard-of-hearing communities, not just for them. The most effective tools support human connection and give people more ways to communicate on their own terms.

## Start with people

Accessibility begins by listening to the people who will use a product. Deaf and hard-of-hearing people have varied communication preferences, including sign languages, captions, text, and speech. A useful experience respects that variety instead of assuming one solution works for everyone.

## Make access part of the experience

Captions, clear visual cues, and flexible ways to participate should be considered from the start. When accessibility is part of the design process, it can improve communication for everyone and help remove barriers before they become entrenched.

## Keep people in control

Technology should make it easier for people to communicate, learn, and participate while keeping their choices and privacy at the center. That means building tools transparently, testing them with the communities they serve, and continuing to improve them based on real feedback.

Inclusive technology is not a replacement for human connection. It is an opportunity to create more ways for people to connect.
